# Real-World Case Study Diagnostics & Design Smells

During Phase 2 of `design-game`, scan the user's input for the following **Design Smells**. When triggered, cite the historical case study and recommend the paired solution.

| Design Smell Detected in User Input | Cautionary Failure Case Study | Benchmark Success Case Study | Actionable Recommendation to Pitch to the User |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. "Loot/Upgrades with Hidden Scaling" or "Unclear Action Feedback"**<br>Player stats increase on paper, or enemies scale invisible HP alongside the player so combat always takes the exact same number of hits. | ***Anthem* (2019)**<br>violated Salen & Zimmerman's **Discernibility & Integration**. Hidden scaling math made the Level 1 starter rifle stronger than endgame Masterwork guns, shattering the Magic Circle. | ***Breath of the Wild* (2017)**<br>used transparent constitutive physics/chemistry rules where fire, wind, electricity, and weapon durability behaved consistently everywhere. | **Fix:** Eliminate invisible rubber-band stat scaling on basic enemies. Make upgrades change *behaviors and capabilities* (e.g., piercing shields, chain lightning, faster recovery frames) rather than invisible percentage math. |
| **2. "Real-Money Trading, Pay-to-Skip Core Loop, or Uncapped Resource Farming"**<br>Players can buy or trade the exact items the core gameplay loop is meant to reward, or resources have sources with no drains. | ***Diablo III* (2012 Launch)**<br>violated Schell's **Lens #2 (Endogenous Value)** and Fullerton's **Economy Balance**. The Real-Money Auction House made playing the dungeon crawler feel less efficient than playing a storefront menu, forcing devs to nerf drop rates to near-zero. | ***Hades* (2020) & *Diablo III: Loot 2.0* (2014)**<br>protected endogenous value by binding progression to active runs and turning every resource into a multi-tier converter with permanent sinks. | **Fix:** Strictly decouple monetization from core power rewards (keep monetization cosmetic or expansion-based). Add late-game **Resource Drains** (consumable modifiers, cosmetic sinks, or prestige resets) so currencies retain scarcity. |
| **3. "Theme/Character vs. Mechanic Dissonance"**<br>The story features unique characters or a distinct dramatic premise, but the mechanics force generic, identical gameplay across all of them. | ***Suicide Squad: Kill the Justice League* (2024)**<br>violated Schell's **Elemental Tetrad Harmony** and Fullerton's **Dramatic Premise**. Four distinct villains (King Shark, Captain Boomerang, etc.) were all forced to play as airborne hitscan rifle shooters chasing "+3% Frost Damage" gear scores. | ***BioShock* (2007) & *Hades* (2020)**<br>achieved total Tetrad Harmony. *BioShock* turned Ayn Rand's philosophy into the Little Sister resource dilemma; *Hades* turned Greek immortality into the literal roguelite respawn mechanic. | **Fix:** Audit every character or mechanic against the **Elemental Tetrad**. If a character's story identity is "stealth infiltrator" or "heavy brawler," their core movement, camera framing, and resource economy must reflect that fantasy rather than sharing a generic weapon template. |
| **4. "Overloaded/One-Button Gimmick Controls" or "Platform-Hostile Input"**<br>Removing core verbs (like jumping or dodging) when equipping items, or mapping 8+ simultaneous twitch buttons onto a mobile touchscreen. | ***Balan Wonderworld* (2021)**<br>violated Rogers' **Three Cs** and Schell's **Make a Toy First**. Mapping every action to a single button meant equipping a combat costume deleted the player's ability to jump in a 3D platformer. | ***Super Mario Odyssey* (2017) & *Astro Bot* (2024)**<br>mastered the **Three Cs** and **Training Cages**. Core movement (jump, dash, hover) remains sacred and responsive while power-ups layer naturally onto two core buttons. | **Fix:** Lock the sacred movement verbs (Move, Jump/Dash, Primary Action) so they are never disabled by equipment states. Design a 60-second **Grey-Box Toy Test** first to prove the character feels great to move in an empty room. |
| **5. "Snowball Runaway / Unbalanced Multiplayer Pacing"**<br>The player who wins Round 1 gets richer/stronger with no comeback vector, or difficulty stays flat for 30 minutes straight. | ** Classic *Monopoly* Runaway / Flat Arcade Grinds**<br>violates Salen & Zimmerman's **Cybernetic Balance** and Schell's **Interest Curves**. Once a player gains an early lead, positive feedback guarantees a slow, boring 45-minute death march for everyone else. | ***Mario Kart 8 Deluxe* & *Left 4 Dead***<br>used distance-based item probabilities (Negative Feedback) and an automated **AI Director** to alternate high-intensity combat peaks with quiet recovery valleys. | **Fix:** Pair every **Positive Feedback Loop** (win streaks/xp bonuses) with a **Negative Feedback Stabilizer** (leader bounties, upkeep taxes, or catch-up drafts) and alternate **Alleys** (low-stress pacing) with **Arenas** (high-stress combat peaks). |
| **6. "8-Year Bubble / No Differentiation / Wall-of-Text Tutorials"**<br>Entering a crowded genre with no unique mechanical hook, muddled character silhouettes, or onboarding via 10 pop-up text modals. | ***Concord* (2024)**<br>failed Schell's **Eight Filters** (Innovation, Demographics, Marketing) and Fullerton's **Early Playcentric Loop**, launching with unreadable character silhouettes and no mechanical hook to justify switching from entrenched competitors. | ***Apex Legends* (2019) & *Slay the Spire* (2019)**<br>*Apex* innovated on implicit rules with its 1-button contextual Ping System and crystal-clear character silhouettes; *Slay the Spire* innovated by telegraphing exact enemy intents each turn. | **Fix:** Run the concept through **Schell's Eight Filters** immediately. Define one unmistakable mechanical hook visible in a 5-second GIF, enforce **Rogers' Silhouette Test** on all characters/enemies, and replace all text tutorials with an interactive **Training Cage**. |
